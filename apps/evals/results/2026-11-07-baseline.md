# Zoom baseline: `v0` and `v1` on ten models, 2026-11-07

Raw results: [`2026-11-07-baseline.json`](2026-11-07-baseline.json) (400 runs, the raw text of every attempt included). Scripts: [`../baseline/`](../baseline/), run as `pnpm baseline:run` from `apps/evals`. View it with `pnpm report results/2026-11-07-baseline.json`, which makes no model call and writes a self-contained HTML page. Every number below is computed from the stored text with the validators of commit `00651f2`, the one after the runs; the runs themselves were made on commit `61715c4` (the file records it, with a clean tree).

## What was run

Every program of the corpus (20, written by hand: 8 S, 7 M, 4 L, 1 XL by block count, with two compositions of unrelated programs and one custom block set) with both frozen prompts, `v0` and `v1`, on ten models, one sample each: 400 runs. Each run follows the proxy's zoom route (up to three attempts, a retry carries the last error, a truncated answer stops it) without the cache. Parameters were the shared ones, `temperature` 0.2 and `maxTokens` 32768, except for the two models that do not support a temperature, which got none. Reasoning was left at each model's default. All calls went through LiteLLM with a provider pin and no fallback.

By how often a model's output was valid, the models fall into three groups. `claude-haiku-5.5`, `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, `glm-5.3-flash` and `minimax-m3` were valid in at least 19 of 20 runs with both prompts. `qwen3.8-27b` and `deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017` were valid in every `v0` run and lost some `v1` runs, and `gemma-4-26b-a4b-it` lost four `v0` and two `v1` runs. `gpt-5.4-nano`, `gpt-oss-20b` and `qwen3-coder-next` were the weakest. The runs that failed ended in invalid JSON (`parse`, mostly `gpt-oss-20b`: 19 of its 40 runs), in output that does not match the schema (`schema`, mostly `qwen3-coder-next` and `gpt-5.4-nano`), or in output that does not fit the Detail workspace (`validation`). Across all models `v0` failed 32 runs (13 parse, 7 schema, 12 validation) and `v1` failed 44 (10 parse, 20 schema, 10 validation, 1 truncated, 3 timeouts).

## H0: does `v0` have failures that small programs hide?

Yes, but not the ones the plan guessed.

- **`v0` produced exactly one Concept block in every valid zoom: 168 of 168**, on every model and every program, from 8-block programs to a 203-block one. It never reached the minimum number of concepts on a composition of unrelated programs: 0 of 16 valid `v0` zooms of the two compositions (3 and 5 independent programs). This is the "usually ONE block" rule of its prompt, and it fails on any program with several stacks, small ones included. For a program with several stacks `v1` gave several concepts (for example `dice-game` 1 against 3, `robot` 1 against 3, `todo-manager` 1 against 5, `inventory` 1 against 4), and 1 for the single-stack programs (`countdown`, `fizzbuzz`), where one is right.
- **Its validity did not fall with size** in this sample: 88% of S runs, 81% of M, 80% of L and 90% of XL (all ten models; XL is one program, so 10 runs).
- **Its input does grow:** the mean first-attempt input, as the providers counted it, was 1.9k tokens on S, 3.3k on M, 6.6k on L and 13.6k on XL for `v0`, against 2.1k, 2.7k, 4.4k and 7.4k for `v1`. `v0` pretty-prints the workspace. For the largest program (`tic-tac-toe`, 203 blocks) the providers counted 11k to 20k input tokens with `v0` and 6.6k to 11.8k with `v1`; the dry run's estimate (four characters a token) was about 30k for `v0`. The token counts here are the providers' own, which differ between models.

## H1: is `v1` better than `v0`?

On the measures that need no judgment, mixed.

- **Better where it was designed to be.** The Concept count was in the author's provisional range in 152 of 156 valid `v1` zooms (97%) against 91 of 168 (54%) for `v0`. All 16 valid compositions had at least as many concepts as the programs they combine (`three-programs`: 3 concepts in all 8 zooms; `five-programs`: 5 or 6 in all 8), where every one of the 16 valid `v0` zooms had 1. The plan `v1` returns covered every top-level stack exactly once in all 156 valid zooms, and none twice.
- **Less often valid.** 156 of 200 `v1` runs ended valid (78%; 79% of the 197 that did not time out) against 168 of 200 for `v0` (84%), and 123 of 200 were valid on the first attempt against 136. The difference is mostly schema failures (20 against 7), concentrated in the weaker models.
- **By model it went both ways.** `v1` helped `gpt-oss-20b` (8 to 13 of 20) and `gemma-4-26b-a4b-it` (16 to 18), and made no difference to `claude-haiku-5.5`, `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` and `glm-5.3-flash` (20 of 20 either way). It hurt `qwen3-coder-next` (10 to 3), `gpt-5.4-nano` (14 to 9), `deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017` (20 to 17, all three in invalid JSON), `qwen3.8-27b` (20 to 17, all three timeouts: 17 of the 17 that finished) and `minimax-m3` (20 to 19, one truncation).
- **More Detail values in labels.** The labels of a valid `v1` zoom held 5.3 values that Detail also holds (D17) against 3.0 for `v0`. `v1` makes more concepts, so it has more labels; the rate per label was not computed.
- **Smaller on the other counts.** Naming problems fell from 0.07 to 0.01 per zoom, and the two levels' label overlap (D18) rose from 0.25 to 0.30. Both levels were smaller than Detail in every valid zoom of both prompts (D13): in blocks, Semantic was 14% of Detail for `v0` and 19% for `v1`, Concept 5% and 9%.
- **Cost and time.** `v1` was cheaper in input and more expensive in output for some models: `glm-5.3-flash` produced twice the output (5.6k to 11.8k tokens) and took twice as long.

So `v1` fixes what `v0` gets universally wrong, the number of concepts, and costs some validity on the weaker models and more labels with Detail values. Whether the abstractions are better is not measured here.

## Other findings

- **Valid zooms that do not load.** 6 of 324 valid zooms (1.9%) had a level that does not load in headless Blockly, that is, the proxy's checks passed it and Blockly refuses it: a `controls_if` missing a connection, a block with no `type`, a block that cannot connect to its parent. The proxy's validation does not look at the built-in blocks it does not know.
- **One truncation:** `minimax-m3`, `v1`, `inventory`, 30,934 tokens against the 32,768 cap. No other run came near it.
- **Three timeouts, kept as timeouts:** `qwen3.8-27b`, `v1`, on `todo-manager` (L), `tic-tac-toe` (XL) and `five-programs` (L). The runner of the time gave up on a call after 600 seconds and repeated it three times, so each run spent 2,473 seconds for no result; a call that fills the 32,768-token cap at that model's speed (about 18 tokens a second) takes about 30 minutes. They count as invalid above and are shown separately where it matters. The runner was fixed afterwards (`00651f2`: a timeout is its own failure kind, is not repeated, and the limit is `--call-timeout`, default 2,400 seconds). Whether OpenRouter billed the aborted calls is not known; the recorded cost shows nothing for them.
- **No transport failures** otherwise: 9 busy answers were repeated and then succeeded. A Blockly warning about `BOOL: true` printed while scoring is only that.

## Limits

- **One sample per cell,** so nothing here says how much a result varies between runs (stability, D19), and the cells are small: 4 L programs and 1 XL program. A difference of one or two runs is not a finding.
- **The corpus is 20 programs written for this baseline,** with no hold-out set, and the expected concept ranges are the author's provisional guesses, agreed but not golden annotations. A valid zoom is not a good one: nothing here measures whether a label is true, whether a behaviour was left out or invented, or whether an abstraction is useful. The golden-annotation metrics (D9, D10, D24), the judge, the human skim, the binding round trip (D7), layout (D25) and the replay gate in CI are not done.
- **Reasoning was at each model's default,** and the defaults differ a great deal (`qwen3.8-27b` defaults to its highest effort, `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` to the lowest), so the models are compared as they behave unprompted, not as they would at the same effort. The two models without a temperature ran without one.
- **The runs are on the code of `61715c4`.** The metrics are recomputed from the stored text each time, so a later validator can change a number here; the stored text does not change.
