# `gpt-oss-20b` under `v2` and `v3`, same day: 2026-11-08 and 09

**Why:** the full `v3` run ([`2026-11-08-v3-baseline.md`](2026-11-08-v3-baseline.md)) had 174 valid runs against `v2`'s 182, and the whole gap was `gpt-oss-20b` (7 of 20 valid under `v3`, 16 of 20 in the `v2` rerun; the other nine models net +1). Two explanations: rate limiting and day-to-day variation of that one model, or a real `v3` effect. This run puts both prompts on the model in the same session.

**Run:** `gpt-oss-20b`, `v2` and `v3`, 20 programs, 2 samples each (80 runs), concurrency 3 (lower than the baseline's 6, to reduce rate limiting), code `0f07b2f` (clean tree). Raw results: [`2026-11-08-gptoss-v2-v3.json`](2026-11-08-gptoss-v2-v3.json). The `v2` and `v3` prompt texts are the ones of their baselines, scored with today's validators.

**Reading fixed before the run:** about equal non-transport failures under both prompts means the gap was rate limiting; `v3` four or more valid runs fewer out of 40 (excluding 429s) means a real effect to read by failure kind.

## Result

|                                                                    | `v2`                                                                 | `v3`                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Valid                                                              | 19 of 40                                                             | 26 of 40                                                             |
| Valid on the first attempt                                         | 4                                                                    | 14                                                                   |
| Failures                                                           | 7 invalid JSON, 5 call failures, 4 truncated, 4 validation, 1 schema | 8 invalid JSON, 2 call failures, 2 validation, 1 truncated, 1 schema |
| Valid of the runs that reached the model (excluding call failures) | 19 of 35 (54%)                                                       | 26 of 38 (68%)                                                       |
| Valid by sample (1, 2)                                             | 10, 9                                                                | 12, 14                                                               |
| Cost, all attempts                                                 | $0.118                                                               | $0.101                                                               |
| Mean time per run, tokens per run                                  | 263 s, 19 675                                                        | 212 s, 16 595                                                        |

**By the reading above, there is no sign of a `v3` effect:** `v3` is not worse, it is seven valid runs better, which on 40 runs is not a finding either way. Rate limiting hit both prompts (5 and 2 call failures).

**The bigger finding is the variation of the model itself.** `gpt-oss-20b` under `v2`: 16 of 20 valid in the rerun, 19 of 40 (48%) today. Under `v3`: 7 of 20 in the full run, 26 of 40 (65%) today. Pooled over both days the two prompts are the same: `v2` 35 of 60 (58%), `v3` 33 of 60 (55%). The spread between days for one prompt is larger than the spread between prompts. Invalid JSON is its commonest failure under both (7 and 8 of 40), and its first-attempt validity swings too (4 of 40 and 14 of 40 today).

**What this does to the full run's C1:** nothing in the table changes, since the criteria were fixed before it and I did not move a threshold. As an observation, not a criterion: without `gpt-oss-20b` the full run has `v2` at 166 of 180 valid and `v3` at 167 of 180. Excluding a model after seeing the result is a choice, so read it as "the gap is this one model", not as `v3` passing.

## Limits

- 40 runs per prompt on one model; two samples of each program, not independent of each other.
- Same day and session for both prompts here, but different days from the full runs; provider load differs between days (the call failures are an example).
- Not a test of whether `v3` helps other models; the other nine were stable between the two baselines.
- My estimates were low: I expected about $0.17 and about 65 minutes. The recorded cost is $0.219, and the summed run times divided by the concurrency (3) come to about 105 minutes (I did not time the run).
